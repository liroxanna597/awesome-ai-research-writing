\section{Conclusion}

This paper proposes and reconstructs a time-aware LLM-driven multi-agent public-opinion simulation framework for public emergencies. Rather than treating public opinion as a purely diffusion-based process, the framework integrates official publisher injection and a cognitive reflection mechanism, emphasizing that official releases, user reflection, and platform exposure jointly shape the trajectory of public opinion. On this basis, we formulate a three-layer mechanism involving official actors, users, and the platform, and validate it on three real cases---the MU5735 crash, the Guangzhou BMW crash incident, and the Cathay Pacific discrimination incident---under both 100-agent and 300-agent settings.

The main findings are as follows. First, the framework performs best on events with moderate emotional intensity, while maintaining low \(\Delta\mathrm{Div}\) and low normalized DTW across all three cases, indicating robust cross-case stability in hourly alignment and temporal-shape matching. Second, the MU5735 case reveals an emotional-ceiling effect of current LLM agents: the simulated sentiment moves in the correct direction, but still fails to reach the extreme intensity observed in real data. Third, scaling from 100 to 300 agents improves \(\Delta\mathrm{Bias}\) in all three cases, supporting the scalability of the framework. Fourth, the ablation results show that reflection and official publishers make independent contributions, but that the direction and magnitude of their effects are moderated by the real emotional baseline of the event.

Overall, the contribution of this work lies not only in building a multi-agent system capable of generating comment text, but also in turning it into a verifiable, replayable, and mechanism-oriented research tool for public-opinion studies. Future work can extend the current framework by adding fuller parameter tables, richer result tables, improved modeling of recommendation dynamics, and stronger calibration of emotional expression in extreme-event scenarios.
